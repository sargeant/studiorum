\section*{Furious Visions}

\subsection*{Drazhan}

The wind screams through the Ashkeeper Peaks. Drazhan stands alone on a narrow cliffside path, barefoot, fists clenched, breathing hard. He’s not a child anymore, but the fury in his chest burns like he is. The memory hits before he can brace himself—the cold sting of rejection, the monastery doors slamming shut behind him. His mother’s voice echoes, gentle but unyielding: \textit{“It’s not safe, Drazhan. You don’t understand what they are.”} He didn’t. He still doesn’t. And in this moment, that \textit{not knowing} twists in his chest like a blade.

The scene shifts with a lurch. Smoke. Screams. Firelight flickering across pale faces and cruel smiles. He sees his father—helpless, bound to a blackened altar. Robed figures chant in a language that scrapes at Drazhan’s memory like claws. A cultist raises a gemstone—deep violet, almost black, pulsing with arcane power. The gem descends with a flash of purple light. And then—nothing. His father is gone. Erased. Drazhan reaches out, but it’s too late. Always too late.

The vision jolts forward again. Time has passed. The monastery lies quiet beneath a moonless sky. Drazhan sees himself far away—older now, scarred and battle-hardened, fighting the cult that shattered his past. He has hunted their remnants across Xhorhas, crushed their cells, shattered their relics, and now finds himself in a great desert city in Marquet. But he is \textit{not here} when they return.

He watches helplessly as they move through the shadows, slipping past old wards and worn stone. His mother, Tasia, hides Miros and Nadia beneath the granary floorboards, her breath shallow, a rusted shovel in her grip. But the cultists don’t find them. Instead, they find \textit{something else}.

A long-buried tunnel has reopened, the ground shifted by time or tremors. The raiders descend into the deep roots of the monastery. The air grows colder, older. They reach a chamber carved in stone, untouched for centuries. At its heart: a sealed door, adorned with a symbol of three eyes carved deep into the stone.

They stare at it, not with reverence, but with hunger. One steps forward, lifting a warpick crowned with a familiar purple gem. He swings it toward the seal.

The eyes blaze without warning. No words, just blinding light. Magic erupts—not fire, not force, but pure, divine insight. The invaders are ash before their screams can leave their throats. The tomb sleeps again.

Drazhan finds himself back in the great hall, chest heaving, jaw clenched. The rage is still there—but it has shape now. Purpose. They came for him once, and took everything. They came again, and the place he tried to flee had \textit{saved} what remained.

\subsection*{Serrik}

The darkness is suffocating—cold, hungry, alive. It wraps around Serrik’s mind like a vice, whispering truths he buried long ago. Then, with no warning, it drags him back.

He stands once more in the blood-soaked marshlands of Brokenveil. The air is thick with smoke and the copper tang of death. His hands grip his blade—coated in fresh blood, his muscles humming with the vicious joy of battle. Around him, bodies sink into the sucking mud, faces frozen in terror. Some wear the red and black of the Dwendalian army. Others wear the same armor he does. The same insignia. \textit{The Aurora Watch.}

But in the thick of it, none of that matters. War made him sharp. Precise. Unstoppable. It needed him. \textit{They} needed him. They cheered when he rode the purple worm into the heart of the enemy. They raised their mugs when his crossbow felled the last of a fleeing battalion. 

Until they didn’t.

The vision twists suddenly—violent, jarring—like a knife driven deep and turned.

Now he stands in Rosohna. The vaulted halls of the Shadowspire echo around him, once filled with applause, now heavy with silence. His commanders sit above, cloaked in shadow and formality. \textit{The war is over}, they declare. \textit{You are no longer required.}

His chest tightens as they strip it all away—his rank, his purpose, his name. Not a soldier. Not a hero. Just another ghost, left behind in the aftermath of peace. 

He pleads. He rages. He begs. But they do not listen.

\textit{“You were useful once,”} one says coldly. \textit{“Now you’re nothing.”}

And then, they turn their backs.

Faces blur in the haze around him, but he knows them—brothers-in-arms, comrades who bled beside him. They do not speak. Do not meet his eyes. Do not defend him. They walk away, and the silence burns hotter than any flame.

The shadows close in again, and from within them, a voice stirs—low and thunderous, a storm beneath his skin.

\textit{“They took everything. Left you to rot. But you were never meant for peace. You are a creature of war. A weapon forged in blood and shadow. Return to what you are. Remind them why they feared you.”}

And for the first time in years, he feels it again—rising from the depths.

Rage. Hot. Relentless. Alive.

\subsection*{Karnik}

The forge heat should be comforting—the hiss of steam, the clang of hammer on steel. But something is wrong. The metal on the anvil glows an eerie violet. Karnik blinks, and the workshop falls away.

Now he stands in snow. Waist-deep drifts coat the path north of Uthadurn, the trees silent beneath heavy frost. Ahead, the clearing. The site.

He knows this moment. He has lived it before.

Blood stains the snow. The missing townsfolk—young, wide-eyed, hopeful—lie dead in the slush, discarded like offerings. Around them, masked figures in flame-marked robes chant in low, guttural tones. The air tastes of ash and metal.

A skirmish breaks out. Karnik charges forward, blade in hand. Cultists fall. Then—he sees him.

Kail.

His brother steps from behind a crumbling pillar of stone, hood thrown back, face as cold as the grave. There’s no remorse in his eyes. No hesitation. Only purpose. His blade is drawn—not to protect Karnik, but to strike him down.

Karnik freezes—not from fear, but from the weight of betrayal. Every late night in the forge. Every long day tracking leads side by side. Every drink shared after cracking a case. It was all real. And still, he chose this.

Kail calls him a fool. Says he was always soft. That the city is rotting, and the fire to come will burn away the weakness.

They fight. Steel clashes, words lost to fury. Karnik is outnumbered, forced to retreat. But on his way out, he grabs it—the glowing heart of the cult’s ritual. A crystalline gem of deep violet flame, pulsing with magic.  

He doesn’t know why he takes it. Only that it feels \textit{wrong}, and \textit{powerful}, and no one else should have it.

The scene bends. Now he is back in Uthadurn—but not in the alleys or the forge. He stands in the offices of the guard. The air reeks of ink and damp wool. Davros Kleggaine stands tall, brows furrowed, hand resting on the hilt of his sword.

\textit{“You’re under arrest for murder.”}

Karnik runs.

Again.

The city that raised him turns its back. His name, once trusted, now spat like poison.

\subsection*{Sunny}


The air shimmers with twilight hues as Sunny stands in the heart of a mystical Feywild forest. The trees stretch impossibly high, their branches twisted with ribbons of soft light and glowing flowers. Time moves strangely here—every breath feels both fleeting and eternal. Her feet don’t touch the mossy ground, yet she drifts forward, pulled toward something unseen but insistent.

Through the emerald mist, a figure emerges.

It’s Olo—her father. His tortle shell catches the soft glow of the forest. His eyes flick back and forth, scanning the shadows. His steps are cautious, deliberate, as if each one brings him closer to danger. He glances over his shoulder, moving quickly yet silently, his urgency unmistakable.

Sunny tries to call out, but her voice fails her. She can only watch.

Olo slips through thick roots and tangled vines, weaving through the ancient forest until he reaches the base of a massive tree. The bark twists and knots around an opening—a jagged archway nearly swallowed by ivy and shadow. Strange symbols are etched into the wood, and the air hums faintly with magic.

From within, the faint metallic ring of something striking echoes, slow and deliberate. The sharp tang of iron and smoke drifts out, cutting through the floral-sweet air of the Feywild.

Olo hesitates at the entrance, his gaze fixed on the darkness beyond. His breath steadies, his jaw tightens. Then, with one final glance behind him, he steps inside.

The vision refuses to follow.

A surge of emotion slams into Sunny, stealing her breath. Her heart clenches as a storm of feelings roars through her.

Dread.

Pain.

Rage.

She doesn’t see what happens, but the truth settles deep in her gut. Something waits inside that place—something that hurt her father, something that still clings to the air like a warning.

Her fists curl as fury builds in her chest, fierce and unrelenting. The vision fades, but the rage refuses to leave her.

The answers lie in the shadows beyond that door.

And whatever waits there will learn the full weight of her wrath.

\subsection*{Chelsynrigg}

The air is heavy—cold and still. Each breath fogs before Chelsynrigg’s face, the swirling frost mirroring that night in the mountains under the Moon Weaver’s gaze. She stands once more at the mouth of the cavern. The shadows inside stretch long and deep, silent and watching.

She knows this place. Knows the pull at the edge of her thoughts—the whisper urging her forward. The shadows ripple, and the stone walls of the cavern twist, reforming into something far more familiar.

A laboratory. Cazryn’s.

The scent of alchemical smoke curls through the air. Cracked glass litters the floor, and scorch marks blacken the walls. It plays out again: the final experiment. Her hands mixing components, trusting instinct. Cazryn’s voice, frantic and sharp—\textit{“No! Don’t—”}

Then the blast.

But this time, the vision holds. Cazryn stands before her—burned, hateful, eyes glowing with arcane fire.

\textit{“You ruined everything,”} she snarls. \textit{“My work. My future.”}

The shadows behind her shift and shape into figures. Her brother. Sunny. Her family. Faces Chelsynrigg knows, all still. All watching.

\textit{“You did this,”} they say in unison. \textit{“You always ruin everything.”}

Her brother steps forward, bitterness carved into every word. \textit{“You dragged me down. You were never enough—not for me, not for them, not for anyone.”}

Cazryn lifts a hand, power crackling between her fingers.

\textit{“The Moon Weaver’s protection is a lie. You’re nothing without that artifact. And you’ll never find it.”}

The weight of their words crushes down. But beneath it, something cracks.

Not with sorrow—with heat.

A spark.

No—a fire.

It rises in her chest, searing away guilt and doubt. Rage—pure, living, electric. The shadows falter. The figures blur. Her own magic flares to life, bright and defiant.

She steps forward.

\textit{“You think I’m nothing?”} Her voice shakes the vision. \textit{“You think you can stop me?”}

\textbf{\textit{“Watch me.”}}

\subsection*{Gulak}


The wind howls across the high steppes, a low mournful cry that pulls Gulak from sleep—or something like it. Snow whips at his face, and he stands once again beneath grey skies, the horizon open and endless. He knows this place. These hills. These winds. This memory.

It begins, as it always does, with trust.

He sees the fire circle—tribesfolk gathered, the smell of roasted meat and tanned hides in the air. Laughter, drink, kin. And among them, the man he once called family. Not by blood, but by bond. A seasoned warrior, a trusted voice, a rider of the plains. Someone who trained with him, hunted beside him, watched the skies together for signs of the Stormlord’s will.

Then the image shifts.

Smoke rises in the distance. Shouting. Stampeding hooves. Gulak runs—barefoot through snow and stone. When he arrives, the camp is in ruin. Tents torn apart. The sacred stones scattered. And worst of all—gone are the relics of his people: heirlooms passed through generations, bound to the very spirit of the tribe. Gone.

Stolen.

He remembers the rage. He remembers the cold certainty when he followed the tracks—hoofprints, heavy and familiar. The trail led not to a rival clan’s warband, but to a meeting. An exchange. Gold. Words in hushed tones. And standing there, sealing the deal with their enemies, was *him*.

The traitor.

Gulak roars, but the vision holds him in place. He watches again as the betrayal unfolds. The look on the traitor’s face—flat, unrepentant. \textit{“It was for survival,”} he had said. \textit{“The old ways are done.”}

The storm above crackles.

Lightning arcs across the sky. And Gulak remembers the mountain.

Remembers the voice that answered him in the wind: \textit{“Go forth as my herald... you will find greater challenges... and one day, you may face me in combat.”}

His chest heaves with fury. Not just for the loss. Not just for the betrayal. But for the \textit{dishonor}. The weakness. The cowardice.

The vision shifts again. He is far away now—standing in a ring, bare-chested, fists raised, blood in his mouth and lightning in his veins. The crowd roars. A beast lies broken at his feet. Somewhere in the back of his mind, thunder claps in approval.

The Stormlord watches.

The wind whispers:

\textit{“Bravery above all. Strength is the path. Glory through battle.”}

Gulak opens his eyes. His hands are shaking. The rage remains.
