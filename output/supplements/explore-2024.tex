\chapter*{Let's Play D\&D 2024!}

\DndDropCapLine{W}{elcome friends. We are here to} play some short adventures using the new \textsc{Dungeons \& Dragons} 5th Edition (2024). With the new edition I wanted to run some smaller adventures to get a feel for the new rules and how they work. I am sure you are eager to try out the new player features and I am keen to see how they work in practice. I am planning a few short adventures for us to play through, and I hope you will enjoy them. So let's get started!

\section{Sourcebooks}

We are using the following sourcebooks for these adventures:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Players Handbook (2024)
    \item Dungeon Masters Guide (2024)
    \item Monster Manual (2024)
    \item Flee Mortals! (MCDM)
\end{itemize}

That's the full list. This means that you can use any of the classes, spells, feats, and other juicy bits from those books. It also means that spells from earlier 5th Edition books are not available, unless they are reprinted in the new books. Sorry, no \textit{Silvery Barbs}.

I chose to include \textit{Flee Mortals!} because I think it is a great book and I want to use some of the monsters from it. I hope you don't mind. Where possible I will be using new stat blocks from the new \textit{Monster Manual} but the odd monster from \textit{Flee Mortals!} may appear because they are just so fun.

\section{The Setting}
The setting is a world of my own creation, a world of magic and mystery, of danger and adventure. The world is a place of great beauty and great danger, a place where the forces of good and evil are in constant conflict.

Actually to be honest, I am not building a deep and intricate world --- I just want to run some fun sessions where you are free to try out the new rules.

\begin{DndTable}[header=Adventure Expectations]{ll}
    Pillar & Amount of content \\
    Exploration & Low \\
    Social Interaction & Low \\
    Combat & High \\
\end{DndTable}

\section{Running the games}

We're not playing a long-term campaign. It's a a few sessions to try out the new rules. I am not going to be too worried about balance, and I am happy to throw some tough encounters at you. My plan is to run one or two sessions for each tier of play.

\begin{DndTable}[header=Tiers of Play]{llX}
    Tier & Level & Description \\
    1 & 1-4 & Local Heroes \\
    2 & 5-10 & Heroes of the Realm \\
    3 & 11-16 & Masters of the Realm \\
    4 & 17-20 & Masters of the World \\
\end{DndTable}

\section{Characters}
My default assumption is that you'll create your own characters using the PHB 2024. If you want to use a character from a previous campaign, that's fine too. I am happy to help you create a character if you need help. 

Because I like using my printed tokens, I will need a picture ahead of time if you have specific character ideas. Otherwise I'll throw down a bunch on the table and you can pick whatever you like.

After each session, you are free to level up your character to the next tier, even if they died. You are also welcome to roll an entierly different character each time we play if you want to try something else. The choice is yours.

\begin{DndTable}[header=Starting Equipment]{cl}
    Levels & Magic Items \\
    1-4 & 1 Common \\
    5-10 & 1 Common, 1 Uncommon \\
    11-16 & 2 Common, 3 Uncommon, 1 Rare \\
    17-20 & 2 Common, 4 Uncommon, 3 Rare, 1 Very Rare \\
\end{DndTable}

\section{Player Character Death}

I am going to try and kill these characters, without being silly and dropping a tarrasque on you in the first session. I want to challenge you, and I want you to feel like you are in danger; because you are. At least one session will end with a TPK.

\section{The Adventures}
I have an embarrasing number of published adventures that I will be pillaging and mashing up to create these sessions. Sometimes it will be contrived, but we're here to play some fun sessions not to make an enduring campaign.

I plan to be more direct with the background and hook. For example; \textit{"You are hired to retrieve the stolen gem from the goblin lair, but you failed your steath check and the entire den is ready to attack"} or \textit{"You are sent to investigate the strange lights in the forest. It turns out you found a portal to the feywild and need to kill an evil hag to get home."}. 

I welcome ideas and requests. Would you like to fight a dragon? How about five dragons and a drunk kraken? What about a cave full of purple worms and a sexy chicken? Let me know and I will see what I can do.
